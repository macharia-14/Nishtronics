A new head unit changes how the whole car feels to use every day. Here is what to check before you buy one.

## 1. Will it fit your dash?

Head units come in two standard sizes, single-DIN (a slim unit) and double-DIN (twice as tall). Many modern units with large screens are double-DIN or a floating screen that mounts on a single-DIN chassis.

Your car also needs the right dash kit and wiring adapter so the unit sits flush and plugs into the factory connectors without cutting wires. Tell us the make, model and year and we will confirm what is needed.

## 2. Phone connection

Most people buy a head unit for this. Check for:

- **Apple CarPlay and Android Auto**, and whether each is wired or wireless.
- **Bluetooth** for hands-free calls and music, which every decent unit has.
- A **USB port** that can charge your phone as well as read it.

Wired CarPlay or Android Auto is simple and charges your phone. Wireless is convenient but costs more.

## 3. The screen

A capacitive touchscreen, like a phone, responds much better than an older resistive one. Look at it in daylight if you can, because some screens wash out badly in bright sun. Resolution and brightness matter more than the diagonal size.

## 4. If you plan to add an amplifier

Check the **pre-out voltage** and the number of pre-out channels. Higher voltage, such as 4 V instead of 2 V, gives the amplifier a stronger signal and usually cleaner sound. Three pairs of pre-outs let you run front speakers, rear speakers and a subwoofer from separate amplifiers.

## 5. Features people forget

- **Steering wheel controls** need a small interface module to keep working.
- A **reversing camera input**, if you want a camera.
- A good **equaliser** and, on better units, time alignment.

## A note on the radio

Many used cars in Kenya are Japanese imports. Their factory radios often cover a narrower FM band than the one local stations broadcast on, so some stations are simply missing. A new head unit with a standard FM tuner brings them all back, and that alone is a good reason to replace an old radio.

## The install matters as much as the unit

A good head unit installed with poor connections will have noise, resets or dead buttons. Done properly, it is a simple job.
