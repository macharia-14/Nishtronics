Most people start with a subwoofer because bass is the easy thing to notice. It is usually not the best first step. If your goal is better sound for the money, this order works.

## 1. Sound deadening on the front doors

It is the least exciting upgrade and often the best value. Door panels are thin and rattle. Treating the inner door skin with deadening material cuts vibration and road noise, and it gives the speaker a solid surface to work against. Mid-bass gets tighter and louder without any extra power.

It also makes the next upgrade sound better, which is why it comes first.

## 2. New front speakers

Factory speakers are usually cheap paper cones. A decent set of replacement speakers, either coaxial or a separate component set with a tweeter, gives clearer vocals and detail straight away. This is where most people hear the biggest difference per shilling.

## 3. A small amplifier for the front speakers

A head unit's built-in power is modest. A compact four-channel amplifier lets the new speakers play loudly without distorting. The sound gets more relaxed, with more headroom, rather than only louder.

## 4. A subwoofer

Now bass makes sense. Adding a subwoofer takes the low notes off your door speakers, which helps them play cleaner. Choose an enclosure to suit your music (we explain the difference in our [sealed vs ported guide](sealed-vs-ported-subwoofer.html)) and use a wiring kit sized for the amplifier.

## 5. Tune it

An unset system sounds worse than a modest one that has been tuned. Setting the amplifier gains, the crossover points and the equaliser properly is what turns a collection of parts into one system. Gain is not a volume knob. Set too high, it clips the signal and damages speakers.

## When the system gets big

Once you add serious amplifier power, the car's electrical system becomes the limit. Thicker power and ground cables, and sometimes a stronger battery or a better alternator, stop lights dimming with the bass. Plan for it in your budget before you buy the biggest amplifier.
