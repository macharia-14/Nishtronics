Buying advice

What a GPS tracker can and cannot do for your car

4 Sep 20262 min readNishtronics team
All articles

A tracker is a small device wired into your vehicle that reports its position over the mobile network. It is worth having if you care about security or if you run a business with several vehicles. It is also easy to expect too much from one.

What a tracker can do

  • Show live location on a phone or computer.
  • Keep a history of trips, stops and routes.
  • Send alerts: when the vehicle leaves or enters an area (a geofence), exceeds a speed you set, starts the ignition, or loses main power.
  • Support a remote cut-off on some models, where a relay stops the engine from being restarted. This has to be wired correctly by the installer.

What it needs

  • A SIM card with data, and mobile coverage where the car is.
  • Access to a tracking platform or app. Some are free with the device and some need a subscription, so ask before you buy.
  • Power from the vehicle. Most devices have a small backup battery, but it only lasts for a limited time.

What it cannot do

  • It cannot work without signal. Underground car parks and some remote areas will show gaps.
  • It can be defeated by a GPS or mobile jammer, which is why hidden installation matters.
  • It will not recover the vehicle by itself. It gives you and the police a position to act on quickly.

Installation is most of the value

A tracker that a thief can find in a minute is not much help. A good installer hides it, powers it from a protected supply and avoids splicing into sensitive wiring such as airbag or data-bus lines. Place and wiring are things you should not leave to guesswork.

Fleet owners

For several vehicles, geofencing, trip reports and ignition alerts let you see who drove where and when. It is also a good way to catch unauthorised use of company vehicles.

Track only what you are allowed to

Fit trackers to vehicles you own, or where the driver knows about it and agrees. That is fair, and it avoids problems later.
